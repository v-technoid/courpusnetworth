Insurance in Canada: What You Have and What to Add

Public cover in Canada, how much life and income cover fits you, and plain pros and cons of each type of insurance. Private, free calculator.

How Cover Works in Canada

Your province pays for doctors and hospitals. The gaps are prescription drugs, dental, vision and income if you cannot work, which is where group benefits and private cover come in.

What You Already Have

Provincial Health Insurance

Each province and territory covers medically necessary doctor and hospital care for residents, such as OHIP in Ontario or MSP in British Columbia.

Official site

Canadian Dental Care Plan

Federal dental coverage for residents without dental insurance and with adjusted family net income under $90,000.

Official site

EI Sickness Benefits

Up to 26 weeks of Employment Insurance at 55% of your earnings, up to $729 a week, if illness or injury stops you working and you have enough insured hours.

Official site

CPP Disability Benefit

A monthly payment if a severe and prolonged disability stops you working regularly, based on your CPP contributions.

Official site
Health cover
Basic care is free, so plan around what is not covered: drugs, dental, eyes, physio and travel outside your province. A workplace benefits plan usually covers these; if you have none, compare individual health and dental plans.

Types of Insurance, Pros and Cons

Term Life Insurance

A tax free lump sum to your beneficiaries if you die during the term.

Pros

  • Low cost for large cover
  • Death benefit is not taxable
  • Easy to match to a mortgage or children's ages

Cons

  • Ends with nothing if you outlive it
  • Renewal premiums jump
  • Permanent policies cost much more

Disability Insurance

Monthly income if you cannot work because of illness or injury.

Pros

  • Protects your income for years
  • Benefits from a policy you pay for yourself are usually tax free
  • Covers illness as well as accidents

Cons

  • Waiting periods of 90 to 120 days are common
  • Group plans may be taxable and limited
  • Expensive for some occupations

Critical Illness

A lump sum if you are diagnosed with a covered illness such as cancer, heart attack or stroke and survive the waiting period.

Pros

  • Money for recovery time or private care
  • Paid whether or not you can work
  • Return of premium options exist

Cons

  • Only listed conditions are covered
  • Premiums are significant
  • Definitions are strict

Supplementary Health and Dental

Pays for prescriptions, dental, vision, physiotherapy and other costs the province does not cover.

Pros

  • Predictable costs for regular care
  • Group plans are often employer paid
  • Covers family members

Cons

  • Annual limits per service
  • Individual plans may exclude pre existing conditions
  • Can cost more than you claim

Travel Medical

Emergency medical costs while outside your province or Canada, which provincial plans cover only partly.

Pros

  • Hospital bills abroad can be enormous
  • Cheap for short trips
  • Often included with credit cards or work plans

Cons

  • Pre existing condition rules are strict
  • Card coverage may have short limits
  • Needs renewing for long stays
