Insurance in the United Kingdom: What You Have and What to Add

Public cover in the UK, how much life and income cover fits you, and plain pros and cons of each type of insurance. Private, free calculator.

How Cover Works in the United Kingdom

The NHS treats you free when you need it, so in the UK insurance is mostly about replacing your income and paying off the mortgage, not hospital bills.

What You Already Have

NHS

Free at the point of use for residents: GP, hospital and emergency care. Prescriptions are free in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and charged per item in England unless you are exempt.

Official site

Statutory Sick Pay

Paid by your employer if you are too ill to work, for up to 28 weeks. Since 6 April 2026 it is paid from the first day of sickness, with no minimum earnings, at the flat weekly rate or 80% of your average weekly earnings if that is lower.

Official site

New Style ESA and Universal Credit

Benefits if illness or disability limits your work for longer, depending on your National Insurance record or household income.

Official site

Death in Service from Work

Many employers pay a lump sum, often two to four times salary, if you die while employed. It stops when you leave.

Official site
Health cover
With the NHS behind you, private medical insurance is a choice about speed and comfort for planned treatment, not a need. Income protection usually matters more, because Statutory Sick Pay is small.

Types of Insurance, Pros and Cons

Life Insurance

A lump sum if you die during the term. Level term keeps the same payout; decreasing term shrinks with a repayment mortgage.

Pros

  • Clears the mortgage and protects your family
  • Cheap when bought young
  • Written in trust, it can be paid out quickly and outside your estate

Cons

  • No payout if you outlive the term
  • Premiums depend heavily on health and smoking
  • Joint policies pay out only once

Income Protection

A monthly income while you cannot work through illness or injury, until you recover, retire or the term ends.

Pros

  • Covers the most likely risk, long illness
  • Pays out repeatedly, not once
  • Benefits are generally tax free when you pay the premiums

Cons

  • Longer waiting periods keep premiums down but delay payment
  • Cheaper policies may only pay for one or two years
  • Check how it treats mental health claims

Critical Illness Cover

A tax free lump sum if you are diagnosed with a listed serious illness such as some cancers, heart attack or stroke.

Pros

  • Money to adapt your home or take time off
  • Often added to a life policy
  • Pays regardless of whether you can work

Cons

  • Only covers the conditions listed
  • More expensive than life cover
  • Severity rules mean some diagnoses do not qualify

Private Medical Insurance

Pays for private treatment of acute conditions, avoiding NHS waiting lists for planned care.

Pros

  • Faster diagnosis and surgery
  • Choice of hospital and specialist
  • Often offered as a work perk

Cons

  • Premiums rise with age
  • Usually excludes chronic and pre existing conditions
  • A work benefit is taxable as a benefit in kind

Family Income Benefit

A form of life cover that pays a monthly income to your family until the end of the term instead of one lump sum.

Pros

  • Often cheaper than a lump sum policy
  • Matches the way families spend
  • Easy to budget around

Cons

  • Pays less the later in the term you die
  • Less flexible than a lump sum
  • Not suited to clearing a mortgage
