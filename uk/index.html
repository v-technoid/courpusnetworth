Tax Year 2026/27 Income Tax at a Glance

6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Rules as of October 2026.

Personal allowance: £12,570, reduced above £100,000.

Taxable incomeRate
£0 to £37,70020%
£37,701 to £125,14040%
Over £125,14045%

Payroll Contributions

  • National Insurance (8%), up to £50,270, above £12,570
  • National Insurance (2% above £50,270), above £50,270
  • Class 4 National Insurance (6%) (self-employed), up to £50,270
  • Class 4 National Insurance (2%) (self-employed)

Rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland sets its own bands for earned income. The personal allowance shrinks by £1 for every £2 earned over £100,000, and is gone at £125,140.

Example: a salary of £45,000 a year.

Income tax£6,486

Total £9,080, an effective rate of 20.2%. Take-home about £35,920 a year, or £2,993 a month.

Self-Employed and Freelancers

  • The first £1,000 of trading income is tax free under the trading allowance.
  • Class 4 National Insurance is 6% of profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above.
  • If your bill is over £1,000, HMRC usually asks for payments on account on 31 January and 31 July.
  • VAT registration is required once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in a rolling 12 months.

On £45,000 of profit, tax and contributions come to about £8,432, so setting aside £703 a month keeps you covered.

Key Dates

  • 31 January: Self Assessment return, balancing payment and first payment on account
  • 5 April: Tax year ends (use your ISA allowance)
  • 31 July: Second payment on account
  • 5 October: Register for Self Assessment if newly self-employed

If Something Goes Wrong

  • Financial Ombudsman Service: Free, independent rulings on complaints about banks, insurers, pensions and investments.
  • Financial Conduct Authority: Check a firm is authorised and report a firm or scam.
  • The Pensions Ombudsman: Disputes about workplace and personal pensions.
  • Citizens Advice: Free advice on consumer problems and next steps.
