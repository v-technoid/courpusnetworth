Insurance in the United States: What You Have and What to Add

Public cover in the USA, how much life and income cover fits you, and plain pros and cons of each type of insurance. Private, free calculator.

How Cover Works in the United States

Most people in the USA get health cover through work, the ACA Marketplace, Medicare or Medicaid. Life and disability cover are up to you, often with a small amount through your employer.

What You Already Have

ACA Marketplace

Health plans you buy yourself on HealthCare.gov or your state's exchange, with premium tax credits for many households based on income. Sign up during open enrollment, which runs from 1 November to 15 January for 2027 cover on HealthCare.gov, or after a qualifying life event such as losing job based cover.

Official site

Medicare

Federal health insurance from age 65, or earlier with certain disabilities. Part A covers hospital stays, Part B doctors and outpatient care, Part D prescriptions.

Official site

Medicaid and CHIP

Free or low cost cover for people and families with low income, and for children through CHIP. Rules differ by state.

Official site

Social Security Disability Insurance

Monthly payments if a long lasting disability stops you working and you have paid enough Social Security tax. The bar is high and the wait is long, so it is a floor, not a plan.

Official site
Health cover
In the USA the number that matters is your plan's out of pocket maximum, the most you pay in a year for covered care. Keep at least that much in your emergency fund. If your plan qualifies for an HSA, money you put in is tax free going in, growing and coming out for medical bills.

Types of Insurance, Pros and Cons

Health Insurance

Pays for doctors, hospital, prescriptions and preventive care, after your deductible and copays, up to the out of pocket maximum.

Pros

  • Caps what a serious illness can cost you in a year
  • Preventive care is usually free
  • Employer plans are paid with pre tax money

Cons

  • Deductibles can be thousands of dollars
  • Networks limit which doctors you can see
  • You can usually only change plans once a year

Term Life Insurance

Pays a lump sum to your family if you die during the term, usually 10, 20 or 30 years.

Pros

  • Cheapest way to protect people who depend on your income
  • Payouts are generally free of income tax
  • Locks in a premium while you are young and healthy

Cons

  • Pays nothing if you outlive the term
  • Premiums rise sharply if you buy late
  • Whole life and universal life policies cost far more for the same cover

Long Term Disability

Replaces part of your income if illness or injury stops you working for months or years.

Pros

  • Protects your biggest asset, your earning power
  • Covers illness, which is the most common cause of long absences
  • Group cover through work is cheap

Cons

  • Waiting periods of 90 days or more are common
  • Benefits are often capped and can be taxable
  • Definitions of disability vary a lot between policies

Umbrella Liability

Extra liability cover above your home and auto policies, typically in steps of one million dollars.

Pros

  • Inexpensive for the amount of cover
  • Protects savings from lawsuits
  • Covers things home and auto policies do not

Cons

  • You must carry high limits on your other policies first
  • Does not cover your own injuries or property
  • Rarely needed with few assets

Long Term Care

Pays for help with daily living at home or in a care facility, which Medicare mostly does not cover.

Pros

  • Care costs can drain savings quickly
  • Hybrid life policies can pay out either way
  • Some states offer partnership plans that protect assets

Cons

  • Expensive, and premiums can rise later
  • Hard to qualify for once health declines
  • Many people never use it
