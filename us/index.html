Payroll Contributions

  • Social Security (6.2%), up to $184,500
  • Medicare (1.45%)
  • Additional Medicare (0.9%), above $200,000
  • Self-employment tax, Social Security (12.4%) (self-employed), up to $184,500
  • Self-employment tax, Medicare (2.9%) (self-employed)
  • Additional Medicare (0.9%) (self-employed)

Federal tax plus an optional flat state rate. State income tax ranges from nothing (Texas, Florida, Washington and others) to over 10% at the top in California. Self-employed people may also get the 20% qualified business income deduction, and families the child tax credit, which are not included here.

Self-Employed and Freelancers

  • Self-employment tax of 15.3% on 92.35% of your net profit covers Social Security and Medicare, and half of it is deductible.
  • Pay estimated tax each quarter (April, June, September, January) if you expect to owe $1,000 or more.
  • Clients usually send a Form 1099-NEC; keep your own records too, since all income is taxable.
  • No federal sales tax or VAT; sales tax is set by each state.

On $75,000 of profit, tax and contributions come to about $17,101, so setting aside $1,425 a month keeps you covered.

Key Dates

  • 15 January: Fourth quarter estimated tax payment
  • 15 April: File your tax return, and first quarter estimated tax
  • 15 June: Second quarter estimated tax
  • 15 September: Third quarter estimated tax
