Payroll Contributions

  • CPF, your 20% (saved for you), up to S$102,000

Resident rates. CPF applies to citizens and permanent residents aged 55 and below, and your contribution counts as relief from tax. No one-off personal income tax rebate is included, Budget 2026 did not announce one for YA 2026.

Example: a salary of S$80,000 a year.

Income taxS$2,160
CPF, your 20% (saved for you)S$16,000

Total S$2,160, an effective rate of 2.7%. Take-home about S$61,840 a year, or S$5,153 a month after savings contributions.

Self-Employed and Freelancers

  • Self-employed people pay tax on net trade income at the same resident rates.
  • If your net trade income is over S$6,000 you must contribute to MediSave.
  • Keep records for five years, since IRAS can ask for them.
  • GST registration is required once taxable turnover passes S$1 million a year.

On S$80,000 of profit, tax and contributions come to about S$3,280, so setting aside S$273 a month keeps you covered.

Common Questions

How much tax do I pay on S$80,000 in Singapore?

For Year of Assessment 2026, about S$2,160 in income tax and payroll contributions, an effective rate of 2.7%, leaving roughly S$61,840 a year. Resident rates. CPF applies to citizens and permanent residents aged 55 and below, and your contribution counts as relief from tax.

What are the Year of Assessment 2026 tax rates?

Rates run from 0% to 24%. The full table is above, and the calculator works out your own figure.

Can I track CPF Ordinary Account and other CPF & SRS accounts?

Yes. Add them under CPF & SRS on the Assets page with your balance and monthly contribution, and they count towards your net worth and retirement projections. CPF: 20% from you and 17% from your employer, up to the S$8,000 monthly ordinary wage ceiling.

Is my financial data uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything is stored only in your own browser on your device. There is no server, account or tracking, and imported documents are read in the page and never kept.
