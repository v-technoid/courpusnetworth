Government Schemes and Benefits in Australia

Savings incentives, family payments, pensions and home buying help in Australia, each with a link to the official page.

Savings and Retirement

Superannuation Guarantee

Your employer must pay 12% of your earnings into super. Since 1 July 2026, under Payday Super, it must reach your fund within 7 business days of each payday instead of quarterly.

Official site

Government Co-Contribution

If your income is under $64,293 in 2026-27 and you make an after tax contribution to super, the government adds up to $500, the full amount below $49,293.

Official site

Low Income Super Tax Offset

If you earn up to $37,000, the government pays back the tax on your concessional super contributions, up to $500 a year.

Official site

First Home Super Saver Scheme

Save for a first home inside super with voluntary contributions, up to $15,000 a year and $50,000 in total, then release them for a deposit.

Official site

Age Pension

A means tested pension from age 67 for people who meet residence rules.

Official site

Family and Income Support

Family Tax Benefit

Fortnightly payments to help with the cost of raising children, based on family income.

Official site

Child Care Subsidy

Pays part of your childcare fees directly to the provider, based on family income. Since 5 January 2026, eligible families get at least 3 days a week subsidised whatever their hours of work or study.

Official site

Paid Parental Leave

Government funded parental leave pay for eligible working parents, with super paid on it for babies born or adopted from July 2025.

Official site

Payment and Service Finder

Check which Services Australia payments you might get.

Official site

Buying a Home

5% Deposit Scheme

Buy with a deposit from 5% (2% for single parents) without lenders mortgage insurance. Since 1 October 2025 there are no income limits or caps on places, and higher property price caps.

Official site

State First Home Buyer Grants and Duty Concessions

Each state and territory has its own grants and stamp duty concessions for first home buyers.

Official site
