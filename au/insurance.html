Insurance in Australia: What You Have and What to Add

Public cover in Australia, how much life and income cover fits you, and plain pros and cons of each type of insurance. Private, free calculator.

How Cover Works in Australia

Medicare covers public hospital care and subsidises doctors. Private hospital cover is optional but has tax rules that push higher earners towards it, and much life cover sits inside super.

What You Already Have

Medicare

Free treatment as a public patient in public hospitals, and rebates for doctor visits and many tests.

Official site

Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme

Subsidised prescription medicines. Since 1 January 2026 a general patient pays at most $25 per PBS script, concession card holders much less, with a safety net for heavy users.

Official site

Insurance Inside Your Super

Most super funds include default death, total and permanent disability, and sometimes income protection cover, paid from your balance.

Official site

NDIS

Funding for supports for Australians under 65 with a permanent and significant disability.

Official site
Health cover
If your income is above the Medicare Levy Surcharge threshold ($105,000 single or $210,000 family for 2026-27) and you have no private hospital cover, you pay an extra 1 to 1.5% tax. Buying hospital cover after the 1 July following your 31st birthday also adds a Lifetime Health Cover loading of 2% a year. Extras cover is optional.

Types of Insurance, Pros and Cons

Private Hospital Cover

Pays for treatment as a private patient, choice of doctor and shorter waits for elective surgery.

Pros

  • Avoids the Medicare Levy Surcharge above the threshold
  • Avoids the Lifetime Health Cover loading if bought early
  • Shorter waits for planned surgery

Cons

  • Gaps and excesses still apply
  • Lower tiers exclude many treatments
  • Premiums rise every April

Extras Cover

Partly pays for dental, optical, physio and similar services.

Pros

  • Useful for regular dental or physio
  • Some funds give free check ups
  • Simple to claim

Cons

  • Yearly limits are low
  • Often costs more than you claim back
  • Does nothing for tax

Life Cover

A lump sum to your family if you die or are diagnosed with a terminal illness.

Pros

  • Often already inside super
  • Cheaper inside super because premiums come from pre tax money
  • Can clear the mortgage

Cons

  • Default super cover may be too low
  • Premiums inside super reduce your retirement balance
  • Cover can lapse if the account goes inactive

Total and Permanent Disability

A lump sum if you are unlikely ever to work again.

Pros

  • Pays for care and home changes
  • Often bundled with life cover in super
  • Large amounts available

Cons

  • Own occupation definitions are rare inside super
  • Claims can take time
  • Tax can apply on payouts from super under 60

Income Protection

A monthly benefit while illness or injury stops you working.

Pros

  • Protects your income, not just a lump sum
  • Premiums outside super are generally tax deductible
  • Covers illness and accidents

Cons

  • Benefit periods and waiting periods change the price a lot
  • Inside super, benefits may stop after two years
  • Self employed income can be hard to prove
